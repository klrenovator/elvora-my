**Dog Collars, Dog Leashes, Dog Collar & Leash Sets** — are three of our seven, so
this is roughly 43% of the catalogue, and it is the part of the business where the
consequences of getting the copy wrong are worst: a badly fitting collar is a
welfare problem, and an unsupported strength claim is a safety problem.

## 2. The sizing problem, stated properly

A dog collar is sold as a neck range, not a single number, because the strap has
holes along part of its length. The buyer's real question is not "what size is my
dog" but **"where in this strap's range does my dog's neck fall?"** If the neck
measures near the top of a size's range, the collar will be almost fully
tightened — the tail sticks out awkwardly and the fit is at the end of its
adjustment. The correct purchase is usually **one size up, with the neck falling
in the middle of the range.**

Mackenzie & George solve this correctly, and the design is worth describing
precisely because it is the model we follow:

1. **Measure** — with a visual adjustment bar showing smallest, recommended and
   largest positions on the strap.
2. **Choose** — a table giving, per size: adjustment range in **inches and
   centimetres**, **strap width**, and a **"recommended fit" band** inside the
   range.
3. **Fit** — allow roughly an inch of slack; the collar should sit high on the
   neck behind the ears on a short-coated dog; two fingers should slide under it.

Verified size data from Mackenzie & George:

| Size | Neck (in) | Neck (cm) | Strap | Recommended (in) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XXS | 8–11 | 20.3–27.9 | 15 mm | 9–10 |
| XS | 10.5–13.5 | 26.7–34.3 | 15 mm | 11.5–12.5 |
| S | 12–16 | 30.5–40.6 | 20 mm | 13–15 |
| M | 14–18 | 35.6–45.7 | 20 mm | 15–17 |
| M Wide | 14–18 | 35.6–45.7 | 25 mm | 15–17 |
| L | 16.5–20.5 | 41.9–52.1 | 25 mm | 17.5–19.5 |
| XL | 19–23 | 48.3–58.4 | 25 mm | 20–22 |

Their two stated principles, both of which we adopt as *guidance* without
copying their wording:

- **Measure, do not buy by breed.** Breed size tables are unreliable; two dogs of
  the same breed can differ by several centimetres.
- **Comfort comes first.** Never tighten a collar to make a size "fit".

### What we implemented, and its provenance

Our sizing guide uses **neck measurement bands in centimetres and inches**, which
are dimensional facts about necks, not proprietary competitor data. It does not
reproduce Mackenzie & George's size labels (XXS…XL), their strap widths, their
recommended-fit values, or their wording. **The specific numbers in our chart are
the owner's to confirm once actual stock is measured** — see the caveat in §5.

## 3. Material and construction claims for pet goods

The eight pet sites collectively imply, but none verifies, the following. Our
copy must not adopt any of them without documentation:

| Claim seen or implied in market | Status | Our rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Escape-proof" | Marketing language, no test standard exists | **Never use** |
| "Chew-proof" / "bite-resistant" | No test standard | **Never use** |
| "Tested to X kg pull" | Not published by any site examined | Only with a manufacturer test report |
| Tensile strength figures | Not published | Only with documentation |
| "Full grain" on a padded or lined collar | Padded collars often use a split or corrected-grain outer | Name the grade only if documented |
| "Handmade" | Common and loosely used | Only if the maker confirms hand construction |
| Hypoallergenic hardware | Meaningless without alloy specification | Name the alloy instead |

**What we may state safely**, because it is descriptive rather than a promise:

- the leather grade (Full Grain / Top Grain) as documented by the maker;
- the strap width in millimetres;
- the hardware finish and metal type if known (e.g. solid brass, plated zinc alloy);
- the buckle type and whether a D-ring is present;
- the adjustment range in cm and inches;
- the stitching type;
- the colour and finish.

That list is enough to make a genuinely informative pet product page. **Nothing
on it requires a strength claim.**

## 4. Pet-specific content gaps we should close

In rough order of value to a buyer:

1. **Sizing guide** — done this phase. Highest value by a wide margin.
2. **How to measure a dog's neck** — included in the guide, with the two-finger
   check and the "measure, don't guess" rule.
3. **Collar vs lead width guidance** — a 20 mm collar on a 40 kg dog is a
   different proposition from a 20 mm collar on a 4 kg dog. We can explain the
   principle (wider strap spreads load over a larger area) **without** claiming a
   load rating.
4. **Lead length, and why it matters** — a 1.2 m lead and a 2 m lead are
   different products. State length; explain the trade-off (control vs freedom).
5. **Hardware care in humidity** — brass tarnishes, plated zinc can pit. In
   Malaysian humidity this is real and worth saying.
6. **Cleaning a collar** — leather collars should not be machine washed. Say so.
7. **Puppy growth** — a puppy-sized collar will need replacing. Honest and useful.
8. **Sets** — explain why a collar and lead in the same leather and hardware
   finish is worth buying together (the finish matches), which is a real reason,
   not an upsell trick.

## 5. The one caveat the owner must resolve

Our sizing guide is written around **neck measurement bands**, and it currently
frames them as the ranges our products cover. **Before launch, the owner must
confirm the actual adjustment range of each collar in stock**, because:

- the range depends on the strap length, the number of holes and where the buckle
  sits — it cannot be inferred from the product's nominal "size";
- if a product's real range differs from the guide, the guide becomes a source of
  wrong orders, which is worse than having no guide.

The guide is therefore written so that **the method is permanently correct** and
**the chart is explicitly flagged as needing confirmation against stock**. A
`TODO` for the owner is recorded in the roadmap. Neither the site nor this
document invents a range for goods that have not been measured.

## 6. What the pet sites do that we deliberately do not

- **Sell harnesses.** Dogs & Horses has a strong step-in harness; several others
  sell them. Outside our seven categories. Not added.
- **Sell I.D. tags.** Mackenzie & George does. Outside our seven categories.
- **Sell poo-bag dispensers and accessories.** Outside our categories.
- **Sell Biothane.** M&G labels it correctly as synthetic — credit to them — but
  our range is leather, and mixing synthetic into a leather catalogue needs the
  owner's decision, not mine.
- **Use breed-size tables.** Unreliable, and M&G themselves warn against relying
  on breed alone.

**The strategic conclusion for our pet range:** we cannot out-range these
specialists, and we should not pretend to. We can be the store that **explains
fit properly, states dimensions precisely, and refuses to make strength claims it
cannot support** — which, on the evidence of all eight sites, is genuinely
differentiating.

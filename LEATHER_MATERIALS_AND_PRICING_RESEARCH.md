# Leather Materials and Pricing Research

**Research date:** 2026-11-21 · Internal document · Not customer-facing copy

Purpose: give the owner a factual basis for product descriptions and pricing, and
give the engineering side a precise boundary for what our copy may and may not
claim.

---

## 1. The grade vocabulary, correctly

The term "genuine leather" is used loosely in the trade. Our catalogue restricts
itself to **Full Grain** and **Top Grain**, which is a deliberate, defensible
position. The definitions below are the standard industry ones and are the only
ones our copy may use.

| Grade | What it is | Physical character | Our position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Full Grain** | The outermost layer of the hide, grain intact, no surface removal | Strongest fibre structure; natural markings and variation are visible and expected | **Sold** |
| **Top Grain** | Outermost layer with the surface lightly sanded or buffed to remove defects | Still the top layer, corrected surface, more uniform | **Sold** |
| **Corrected Grain** | Top grain heavily sanded and often embossed with a printed pattern | Uniform, less breathable, grain is artificial | Not sold |
| **Split Leather** | The lower fibrous layer left after the top is removed | Weak surface layer, often coated | Not sold |
| **Bonded / reconstituted** | Leather fibres glued and pressed | Not a hide structure at all | Not sold |

**The rule our copy must obey, restated because it is the easiest one to break
by accident:** a corrected-grain or split product may never be described as
"full grain", and the softer phrase "genuine leather" must not be used as a
substitute for naming the grade. If a supplier cannot document the grade, the
product description says so rather than guessing upward.

**What we may never claim without manufacturer documentation:** country of
origin of the hide (as distinct from country of manufacture), the tanning agent
(chrome / vegetable / combination), tannery identity, animal breed, thickness in
millimetres, tensile strength, and any dye-fastness or water-resistance figure.

## 2. Tanning, and why it matters to a Malaysian customer

Three methods appear in the market; only the first two are plausible for the
goods in our categories.

- **Vegetable / veg-tan** — tannins from bark and plants; firm at first, darkens
  with light and use; the classic belt and collar leather. Ages visibly.
- **Chrome tan** — fast, soft, uniform, the bulk of the world's leather; most
  soft handbags and wallet interiors.
- **Combination tan** — both, common in bridle leather.

**Why this matters here:** Malaysia is hot and humid year-round with high
average relative humidity. Vegetable-tanned leather is more susceptible to
mould and water staining in this climate; chrome-tanned leather is more
resistant but less characterful. Any care guidance we publish must therefore
address humidity and storage honestly — "keep it dry" is not sufficient advice
in Klang Valley.

**Care copy we can defend without manufacturer testing:**

- Store in the supplied dust bag, away from direct sun.
- Do not store in a closed, damp space; allow air movement.
- Wipe with a dry cloth; avoid soaking.
- Condition sparingly; over-conditioning softens structure.
- Expect the leather to darken and take on a patina — this is normal, not a fault.

**Care copy we may not write:** specific conditioner brands as required, any
lifespan figure in years, any "waterproof" claim, any mould-resistance claim.

## 4. Discounting: what the Malaysian market is teaching customers

| Site | Observed depth |
| --- | --- |
| Leather Avenue | up to **−61%**, permanent-looking countdown timer |
| BONIA | Year-End Sale up to 40% + extra 10% |
| Braun Büffel MY | End of Season up to 30%; 20% on FORME |

Three of three Malaysian competitors run deep, time-limited markdowns with
countdown pressure. This is the market norm and it works — but it trains
customers to wait, and it makes the reference price untrustworthy.

**Recommendation.** Do not run a permanent sale. If a markdown is used at all, it
should be dated, genuinely limited, and the struck "was" price should be a price
the goods actually sold at. A store that never lies about prices can charge more
than one that always does. This is the opposite of what Leather Avenue does, and
it is a defensible position for a small brand.

## 5. Construction details worth describing, and the words for them

Where the owner can confirm them from the maker, these are the specifics that
differentiate a real leather product page from a generic one:

- **Stitching** — machine vs hand; thread type; stitch density (stitches per inch).
- **Edge finish** — burnished, painted, or raw. Painted edges crack; burnished
  edges age.
- **Lining** — full leather, split, or textile. This is where cost is hidden.
- **Hardware** — solid brass, plated brass, or zinc alloy. Plating wears through.
- **Reinforcement** — whether the belt has a leather or synthetic core.
- **Skiving** — whether wallet pockets are thinned at the fold.

**These are questions for the manufacturer, not claims to publish.** Any of them
that the maker documents should go into the product description; any that cannot
be documented should be omitted rather than filled with a plausible-sounding
generalisation. This is the single highest-value thing the owner can do for the
product pages.
